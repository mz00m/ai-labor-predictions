# HR & People Management — Human Capabilities

## Coaching & Individual Development

> Helping people grow — seeing their potential, naming their blind spots with care, and creating the conditions for them to level up.

- **Function**: people-management
- **Also relevant to**: education, sales
- **Why appreciating**: AI can track performance metrics, generate development plans, and even provide feedback on writing or presentations. It cannot sit across from someone, sense that they're stuck because they don't believe they belong in the role, and find the exact words that unlock them. Coaching is the highest-leverage people skill because it multiplies the output of everyone it touches. As AI handles more routine management (status updates, scheduling, reporting), the time freed up for genuine coaching is the single biggest opportunity in management.
- **How to develop**: Learn one coaching framework (GROW is the simplest: Goal, Reality, Options, Will) and use it in every 1:1 for a month. Practice asking questions instead of giving answers. After each coaching conversation, write one sentence about what the person needs most right now. Read "The Coaching Habit" by Michael Bungay Stanier — it's short and immediately applicable.

## Conflict Resolution & Difficult Conversations

> Addressing interpersonal tension, performance issues, and team dysfunction directly and constructively.

- **Function**: people-management
- **Also relevant to**: operations, customer-service
- **Why appreciating**: AI cannot fire someone with dignity. It cannot mediate a conflict between two engineers who respect each other's work but can't stand being in the same meeting. It cannot tell a high performer that their behavior is toxic in a way that they actually hear. These conversations are the hardest part of management and the part that matters most. As AI removes the excuse of "I was too busy with admin to address this," the expectation for managers to handle people issues directly will only increase.
- **How to develop**: Practice the SBI framework for feedback: Situation, Behavior, Impact. Role-play difficult conversations with a trusted peer before having them for real. After each difficult conversation, debrief: what landed, what deflected, what would you change? Read "Crucial Conversations" and apply the STATE framework. Build a reputation as someone who addresses issues early and fairly.

## Team Design & Culture Shaping

> Building teams that work well together — hiring for complement, not similarity, and creating norms that make collaboration natural.

- **Function**: people-management
- **Also relevant to**: operations, technology
- **Why appreciating**: AI can screen resumes and predict job performance from assessments. It cannot design a team where the cautious analyst balances the aggressive salesperson, where the new hire's energy lifts the veteran's cynicism, or where the remote members feel as included as the in-office ones. Team chemistry is emergent, not engineered — but the manager who cultivates it consistently produces outsized results.
- **How to develop**: Map your current team's working styles (not personality types — working styles: how they communicate, make decisions, handle conflict). Identify the gaps: who challenges groupthink? Who keeps the team focused? When hiring, write down what the team needs before you write the job description. Create one team ritual that builds genuine connection (not forced fun — something that surfaces real thinking).

## Change Leadership & AI Transition Management

> Guiding teams through the psychological and operational transition of integrating AI into their work — without losing trust, talent, or morale.

- **Function**: people-management
- **Also relevant to**: operations, communications
- **Why appreciating**: This is the capability of the moment. Every organization is going through AI-driven change, and most are handling it badly: vague reassurances, top-down mandates, or silence. Research shows AI's effect on boosting demand for human leadership and adaptability skills is nearly twice as large as its effect on replacing tasks. The manager who can honestly address fears ("some tasks will change, here's how we're preparing"), create safe experimentation spaces, and help individuals find their new value proposition in an AI-augmented workplace — this person is the difference between a successful transition and a talent exodus.
- **How to develop**: Have individual conversations with each team member about their concerns about AI — listen without reassuring. Be honest about what you know and don't know. Create a "safe experimentation" zone: one hour per week where team members can try AI tools without performance pressure. Document how roles are evolving (not just what's being automated, but what new capabilities are emerging). Study change management frameworks (Kotter's 8 steps, Bridges' transitions model) but don't follow them rigidly — adapt to your team.

## Performance Calibration & Fairness

> Evaluating people accurately and fairly — separating signal from noise, managing bias, and making consequential decisions about careers.

- **Function**: people-management
- **Also relevant to**: legal, finance
- **Why appreciating**: AI can track output metrics. It cannot assess whether someone is delivering less because they're coasting or because they're investing in a project that will pay off next quarter. It cannot weigh the contribution of the person who makes everyone else better against the individual contributor with higher personal metrics. As AI-driven analytics give managers more data, the ability to interpret that data with human judgment — not just optimize it — becomes the critical management skill.
- **How to develop**: Before every performance review, write your assessment without looking at any metrics. Then compare to the data. Where do they diverge? That's where your judgment is being tested. Study the research on bias in performance evaluation (gender, recency, halo effect) and build specific countermeasures into your process. Seek calibration: discuss borderline cases with peer managers before finalizing ratings.
