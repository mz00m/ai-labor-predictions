# Education & Training — Human Capabilities

## Adaptive Teaching & Real-Time Responsiveness

> Reading a room of learners and adjusting in the moment — speeding up, slowing down, switching approaches when the planned lesson isn't landing.

- **Function**: education
- **Also relevant to**: people-management, communications
- **Why appreciating**: AI tutors can personalize content delivery at scale. They cannot see that the student in the third row is confused but too embarrassed to ask, or that the energy in the room dropped because the example didn't connect. The evidence now shows this matters for outcomes, not just atmosphere: in a field experiment with roughly 1,000 high school math students (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025), an unconstrained GPT-4 tutor raised in-session performance 48% but left students worse off once access was removed, while a version constrained to teacher-designed hints raised performance 127% without the same skill loss. The teacher who reads the room and adapts — deciding when to withhold the answer, when to pivot from lecture to discussion — is supplying the judgment the tool cannot.
- **How to develop**: Record yourself teaching (even just screen recordings of virtual sessions) and review for moments where you missed signals or where a pivot worked. Practice having three versions of every key concept ready: technical, analogy-based, and visual. Build a repertoire of "back pocket" activities for when the plan isn't working. If your students use AI tutors, specify what the tool should withhold — hint-only modes are now standard in most classroom AI products, and configuring them is part of the teaching. After each session, note: what did students actually learn vs. what I planned to teach?

## Mentorship & Individual Potential Recognition

> Seeing what a student or trainee could become — and investing in their growth with the patience, challenge, and belief that catalyzes transformation.

- **Function**: education
- **Also relevant to**: people-management
- **Why appreciating**: AI can identify learning gaps through assessment data. It cannot see the student who is underperforming because they don't believe they belong, or the one who is bored because the material is too easy. The strongest current evidence is that AI works best when a human mentor stays in the loop: Stanford's Tutor CoPilot RCT (700+ tutors, 900+ students) raised topic mastery about 4 percentage points, with the largest gains among the lowest-rated tutors, and the World Bank's Nigeria RCT found 0.31 SD overall gains in six weeks in a design where teachers acted as guides rather than bystanders. Note that a widely repeated claim — that AI's boost to demand for mentorship skills is "nearly twice" its displacement effect — overstates the source. Mäkelä and Stephany's job-postings study finds AI roles roughly twice as likely to demand complementary human skills, and complementarity effects larger than substitution effects, but it measures resilience, agility, and analytical thinking, not mentorship. The educator who recognizes potential the learner doesn't see, and finds the right combination of challenge and support to unlock it, is doing the work AI keeps making more valuable.
- **How to develop**: For each student or trainee, write one sentence about what they could achieve that they don't yet believe is possible. Practice the "warm demander" approach: high expectations communicated with genuine care. Study Carol Dweck's growth mindset research — not the pop version, the actual research on how belief about capability affects learning. Build individual check-ins into your practice: 5 minutes of genuine conversation about goals, not just performance. If you supervise tutors or junior instructors, treat AI coaching tools as a floor-raiser for weaker mentors, not a substitute for your own.

## Learning Experience Design

> Crafting educational experiences that create genuine understanding — not just information transfer, but the "aha moment" where concepts click.

- **Function**: education
- **Also relevant to**: marketing, creative-design
- **Why appreciating**: AI can generate curriculum, practice problems, and explanations. It cannot decide what to withhold. That decision now has measurable stakes: in Bastani et al. (PNAS 2025), two AI tutors built on the same model produced 48% versus 127% performance gains depending purely on instructional design, and the unconstrained version left students weaker when the tool was taken away. Design choices — where the productive struggle sits, how concepts sequence, what the project makes urgent — are the variable that determines whether AI-assisted instruction produces learning or the appearance of it. Institutions are acting on this: the University of Surrey rebuilt its curriculum and assessment policy around process rather than output effective September 2026, and Bath moved to a principles-based "two-lane" model for 2026-27.
- **How to develop**: Study learning science: spaced repetition, interleaving, retrieval practice, desirable difficulties. Design one "discovery-based" activity per unit — where students encounter the problem before the solution. For any AI tool you put in front of students, write down explicitly what it must not do. A/B test your approaches: try two different ways to teach the same concept and compare comprehension, including comprehension measured without tool access. Read "Make It Stick" by Brown, Roediger, and McDaniel for evidence-based learning strategies.

## Assessment Integrity & Meaningful Evaluation

> Evaluating learning in ways that resist AI circumvention and actually measure understanding — not just information recall.

- **Function**: education
- **Also relevant to**: people-management, technology
- **Why appreciating**: Unsupervised take-home work no longer certifies individual capability. In HEPI's 2026 UK undergraduate survey (n=1,054), 94% reported using generative AI for assessed work, and the share pasting AI-generated text directly into submissions rose to 12%, from 8% in 2025 and 3% in 2024; 65% said assessment has already changed significantly in response. Pew found 10% of US teens use AI for all or most of their homework. Detection is not the answer — AI text detectors remain unreliable across unseen models and adversarial rewriting and should not serve as sole evidence of misconduct. That leaves assessment design as the only durable control, and the educator who can build oral examinations, live problem-solving, portfolio defense, and traceable collaborative work is holding up institutional credibility.
- **How to develop**: Redesign one assessment this term to be AI-resistant: oral component, live demonstration, or portfolio defense. Voice-AI-administered oral assessments have made this cheap enough to scale — one undergraduate deployment ran 36 oral exams for about $0.42 per student — but the rubric and the follow-up questions still have to be yours. Never treat a detector score as evidence; authenticate through your own knowledge of the learner's work. Build rubrics that evaluate thinking process, not just output. Accept that some AI use in learning is beneficial — the skill is knowing which competencies must be demonstrated AI-free.
