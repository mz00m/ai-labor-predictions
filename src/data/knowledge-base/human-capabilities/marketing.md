# Marketing & Brand — Human Capabilities

## Brand Intuition & Taste

> The ability to feel whether something is on-brand without checking a style guide — and to evolve the brand without breaking it.

- **Function**: marketing
- **Also relevant to**: creative-design, communications
- **Why appreciating**: AI generates infinite content, but it has no taste. It can match patterns but can't feel when something is trying too hard, or when a subtle shift in tone would land better. A major study of 12 million job postings found that the more AI a company adopts, the more it seeks people with taste, creative judgment, and the ability to tell good from great. As AI floods every channel with competent-but-generic content, the human with genuine taste becomes the signal in the noise.
- **How to develop**: Build a swipe file of marketing you genuinely admire (not just "best practices" lists). For every piece your team produces, write one sentence about what makes it distinctly yours vs. what any brand could say. Study brands that maintained identity through decades of change (Apple, Patagonia, The Economist). Practice editing AI output: what do you cut, add, or reword?

## Audience Empathy & Cultural Context

> Understanding what your actual audience cares about, fears, and aspires to — not what the data says they should care about.

- **Function**: marketing
- **Also relevant to**: sales, communications, customer-service
- **Why appreciating**: AI can segment audiences and predict click-through rates. It can't sit in a customer's kitchen and understand why they chose your product over the cheaper alternative. As personalization becomes table stakes, the marketer who truly understands the emotional landscape of their audience creates campaigns that AI-generated content can't match.
- **How to develop**: Do 5 customer interviews this quarter with no agenda other than understanding their world. Read the forums, subreddits, and comment sections where your audience talks when they don't know you're listening. Build customer journey maps that include emotional states, not just touchpoints. Test your assumptions: write what you think customers want, then validate it.

## Strategic Narrative Construction

> Building the story that connects product capabilities to human outcomes — the "why should I care" bridge.

- **Function**: marketing
- **Also relevant to**: communications, sales
- **Why appreciating**: AI can write blog posts and social copy. It cannot construct the strategic narrative that makes a company matter — the through-line from problem to solution to worldview that turns customers into advocates. This is architectural thinking applied to language.
- **How to develop**: Study how the best companies frame themselves (not their products — their role in the world). Practice writing the same product benefit three ways: feature-first, benefit-first, worldview-first. Read "Building a StoryBrand" by Donald Miller and apply the framework to your company. Write your company's narrative in 100 words, 50 words, and 10 words.

## Channel Judgment & Resource Allocation

> Knowing which channels actually drive value vs. which just generate activity — and having the conviction to say no.

- **Function**: marketing
- **Also relevant to**: operations, finance
- **Why appreciating**: AI makes it trivially cheap to be present on every channel. The temptation to do everything intensifies. The marketer who can say "we're cutting three channels and doubling down on two" — and be right — is more valuable than ever. Focus is a competitive advantage when everyone else is spraying AI-generated content everywhere.
- **How to develop**: Audit your current channels: which ones drive pipeline, which drive vanity metrics? Run a "channel fast" — stop posting on your weakest channel for 30 days and measure the actual business impact. Build an attribution model that you actually trust (even a simple one). Practice saying no to new channel opportunities with a one-sentence rationale.

## Cross-Functional Translation

> Translating between engineering, sales, product, and executive language — making marketing the connective tissue of the organization.

- **Function**: marketing
- **Also relevant to**: operations, communications, people-management
- **Why appreciating**: As AI handles more production work, marketing's value shifts from making things to connecting things. The marketer who can sit in a product roadmap meeting, extract the story, align it with sales messaging, and brief the exec team — all in the same day — is operating where AI can't.
- **How to develop**: Attend one meeting per month outside your function (engineering standup, sales pipeline review, customer support retrospective). Build a glossary of how different teams describe the same things differently. Practice translating: take a technical spec and write the customer-facing story in 15 minutes.
