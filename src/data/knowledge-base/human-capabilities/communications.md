# Communications & Public Relations — Human Capabilities

## Crisis Communication & Reputation Management

> Responding to reputational threats with speed, judgment, and authenticity when the stakes are highest.

- **Function**: communications
- **Also relevant to**: people-management, legal
- **Why appreciating**: AI can draft holding statements and media responses. It cannot read the temperature of a crisis: whether silence is better than a response, whether the CEO needs to speak personally, whether the apology should be contrite or defiant. There is now experimental evidence that the human element is doing real work. Studies published in 2025-2026 in the crisis communication literature — including in Corporate Reputation Review and the Journal of Public Relations Research — find that an identical crisis statement attributed to a human draws higher trust, credibility, forgiveness intention, and reputation ratings than the same statement disclosed as AI-generated. Apology strategies mitigate damage, but noticeably less so when the source is a machine. One wrong move amplifies the crisis, and the human who navigates it with genuine judgment is the most valuable person in the organization when it matters most.
- **How to develop**: Study five major corporate crises from the last three years. For each, map the timeline: first signal, first response, escalation, resolution. What worked, what made it worse? Build a personal crisis communication framework: who speaks, when, through which channels, with what approval chain. Decide the AI-disclosure question before you are in a crisis — how much AI assistance you will use in crisis materials and how you will disclose it — because the source-attribution research says that choice moves trust. Practice writing three different responses to the same hypothetical crisis and defend each approach.
- **Automation resistance**: crisis-adaptation, judgment-under-ambiguity, ethical-reasoning
- **Task categories**: communication, interpersonal, analysis-decision

- **Confidence**: high

## Authentic Voice & Tone Calibration

> Finding and maintaining a genuine organizational voice that resonates with audiences — and knowing when to shift tone for different contexts.

- **Function**: communications
- **Also relevant to**: marketing, creative-design
- **Why appreciating**: AI-generated content sounds competent but generic, and audiences have started pricing that in. Gartner's 2026 consumer research finds roughly half of consumers prefer to give their business to brands that keep AI out of consumer-facing content entirely, and 68% say they frequently wonder whether what they see online is real. Peer-reviewed work on the AI disclosure penalty finds the same pattern experimentally: labeling content as AI-generated raises perceived novelty but lowers perceived authenticity and trust, with the authenticity effect dominating. Meanwhile PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer, drawing on more than a billion job postings, finds employers weighting judgment, creativity, and leadership more heavily as AI adoption rises. The communicator who can write in a way that sounds like a real human with real convictions — and can shift that voice for an investor letter vs. an all-hands email vs. a press release — is doing work that is getting scarcer and more valuable at the same time.
- **How to develop**: Read your organization's last 10 external communications. Highlight every sentence that could have come from any company. That's the AI-replaceable content. Now find the sentences that could only come from your organization. A useful forcing function: generate the same piece with a model, then delete from your draft everything the model also produced. Study how great writers maintain voice across contexts (Warren Buffett's shareholder letters are the gold standard). Practice writing the same message for three audiences in three different voices.

- **Task categories**: communication, creative-generative
- **Appreciation score**: 9
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: high

## Stakeholder Sensing & Narrative Timing

> Knowing when a message will land and when it will backfire — reading the room across audiences, news cycles, and organizational moods.

- **Function**: communications
- **Also relevant to**: marketing, people-management
- **Why appreciating**: AI can schedule content optimally for engagement. It cannot sense that announcing a hiring freeze the same week as record earnings will create a trust crisis, or that the competitor's bad press creates a window for a thought leadership piece. Timing judgment is cultural intelligence applied to communication — and it compounds with experience.
- **How to develop**: Build a "news radar" habit: 15 minutes each morning scanning your industry, your competitors, and the broader cultural moment. Before sending any major communication, ask: "What else is happening right now that changes how this will be received?" Keep a log of timing decisions (both good and bad calls) and review quarterly. Develop 2-3 trusted advisors you can gut-check timing with.

- **Task categories**: communication, analysis-decision

## Relationship Journalism & Media Fluency

> Building genuine relationships with journalists, analysts, and influencers — understanding how media works from the inside.

- **Function**: communications
- **Also relevant to**: marketing, sales
- **Why appreciating**: AI can write press releases and pitch emails. It cannot build the relationship where a journalist calls you for a quote because they trust your expertise and candor. Cision's 2026 State of the Media report, surveying 1,899 journalists in early 2026, quantifies the shift: 53% now oppose AI-generated pitches on accuracy and personalization grounds, and 43% name AI as one of the biggest challenges the industry faced in the past year, up from 30% the year before. At the same time journalists' dependence on communicators has grown — 66% rely on PR-provided material for story ideas, making PR their leading source of leads. As AI-generated pitches flood inboxes, the communicator with genuine relationships becomes the bottleneck for earned coverage.
- **How to develop**: Identify 10 journalists who cover your space. Read everything they've written in the last 6 months. Understand their beat, their angle, their frustrations. Reach out with one useful insight or data point, not a pitch — 84% of journalists in the Cision survey say the best way to start a relationship is a plain introduction email with no story attached, explaining why you want to connect. Be the source who makes their job easier: responsive, quotable, honest about limitations. Build the relationship before you need it.

- **Task categories**: interpersonal, communication
- **Appreciation score**: 9
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: high

## Internal Communication Architecture

> Designing how information flows inside the organization — what people need to know, when, and through which channels.

- **Function**: communications
- **Also relevant to**: people-management, operations
- **Why appreciating**: As organizations become more distributed and AI handles more routine coordination, the design of internal communication becomes a strategic capability. The communicator who understands that the all-hands email isn't landing, that the Slack channel is creating information overload, and that the quarterly business review needs a different format — this person shapes organizational effectiveness.
- **How to develop**: Audit your organization's internal communication: what do people actually read? What gets ignored? Survey 10 colleagues about their information diet — what's useful, what's noise? Propose one experiment: kill one communication channel or format for a month and measure what happens. Study organizations known for communication excellence (Stripe's writing culture, Amazon's memo format).
