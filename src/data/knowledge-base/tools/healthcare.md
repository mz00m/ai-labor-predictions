# Healthcare & Medical Practice — AI Tools

## Nuance DAX Copilot

- **Function**: healthcare
- **Also useful for**: administrative
- **Pricing**: Paid (enterprise quote-only, negotiated per health system; no published list price)
- **URL**: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/health-solutions/clinical-workflow/dragon-copilot
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: low

> Discontinued as a standalone product. DAX Copilot was merged with Dragon Medical One in March 2025 and now ships as Microsoft Dragon Copilot; the ambient documentation capability described here lives inside that product.

**Best for:** Health systems and large practices looking to reduce clinician documentation burden. The ambient capability captures the natural patient-provider conversation and drafts encounter notes into the EHR, letting physicians review and sign off rather than type from scratch. Particularly strong in primary care and specialty visits with high note volume.

**Limitations:** The DAX Copilot name and its standalone SKU are retired — evaluate Microsoft Dragon Copilot instead, and treat any DAX-era pricing or contract terms as obsolete. New procurement routes through Microsoft enterprise sales rather than legacy Nuance channels, and existing DAX contracts migrate to Dragon Copilot licensing at renewal. Reported per-provider rates vary widely by channel and volume, so a quote is the only reliable number. No self-serve signup for individual providers. Integration depends on EHR compatibility. Clinicians must review every generated note before signing, so time savings vary by specialty.

## Nabla

- **Function**: healthcare
- **Also useful for**: administrative, communications
- **Pricing**: Paid (quote-only; vendor no longer publishes rates, third-party reports ~$119-$239/provider/mo)
- **URL**: https://www.nabla.com
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: medium

> AI copilot for clinicians that auto-generates visit notes, patient summaries, and follow-up communications from recorded conversations.

**Best for:** Small to mid-size practices, and increasingly health systems, that need ambient documentation without a Microsoft-scale enterprise agreement. Nabla handles visit note generation, after-visit summaries, and drafts of patient messages, with ICD-10 and HCC code suggestions. Native integrations now include Epic and Oracle Cerner alongside athenahealth, NextGen, and Greenway, and Nabla Connect lets EHR vendors embed the module directly. Works well for providers doing a mix of in-person and virtual visits.

**Limitations:** Pricing is no longer published — expect a demo and a sales conversation rather than self-serve signup, which slows evaluation for solo practitioners. Note quality can vary for highly specialized medical terminology outside common primary care and behavioral health contexts. Providers should verify BAA coverage and data handling policies match their own compliance requirements rather than relying on vendor marketing claims.

## Freed

- **Function**: healthcare
- **Also useful for**: administrative
- **Pricing**: Paid ($39/mo Starter for 40 notes, $79/mo Core unlimited, $104-119/mo Premier)
- **URL**: https://www.getfreed.ai
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: high

> AI medical scribe designed for independent practitioners and small practices, generating SOAP notes from patient conversations.

**Best for:** Solo practitioners and small practices that cannot afford a human scribe or an enterprise ambient documentation system. Freed listens to encounters and produces structured SOAP notes that clinicians can edit and finalize. The lower price point and straightforward onboarding make it accessible for practices without dedicated IT staff. A 7-day trial requires no credit card, and residents, students, and trainees can get the Core tier free. Group plans with shared notes, MA seats, SSO, and admin dashboards are quoted separately.

**Limitations:** Less customizable than enterprise solutions — note templates and specialty-specific formatting may not match every workflow. The entry Starter tier caps at 40 notes per month, so most full-time clinicians need Core or above. Integration with EHR systems is shallower than Dragon Copilot or Elation; many users copy-paste notes rather than having them flow directly into the chart. As with all clinical AI scribes, every note requires physician review to catch errors, and accuracy drops in noisy environments or with heavy medical jargon.

## Elation Health

- **Function**: healthcare
- **Also useful for**: administrative, operations
- **Pricing**: Paid (quote-based, from ~$349/provider/mo; ~$399 with billing/insurance features)
- **URL**: https://www.elationhealth.com
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: medium

> EHR platform with built-in AI clinical assistant for documentation, coding suggestions, and patient messaging.

**Best for:** Primary care and direct primary care practices that want AI capabilities embedded natively in their electronic health record rather than bolted on. Elation's AI assists with clinical note drafting, suggests appropriate billing codes based on documentation, and helps generate patient-facing messages — all within the same system clinicians use for charting and ordering.

**Limitations:** Primarily serves primary care; specialists with complex workflow requirements may find it limiting compared to specialty-specific EHRs. The all-in-one approach means switching to Elation requires an EHR migration, which is a significant lift for established practices — budget for implementation (commonly ~$2,000-$5,000) and data migration (~$2,000-$15,000 depending on record volume) on top of the subscription. Final pricing is quoted per practice, so the published starting figure is a floor. AI coding suggestions are assistive only — providers must verify code accuracy to avoid compliance and billing risks.

## Regard

- **Function**: healthcare
- **Also useful for**: operations
- **Pricing**: Paid (enterprise quote-only; no published per-provider price)
- **URL**: https://regard.com
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: medium

> AI diagnostic assistant that automatically reviews patient charts and surfaces potential diagnoses clinicians may have overlooked.

**Best for:** Hospital medicine and inpatient settings where diagnostic completeness directly affects patient outcomes and accurate clinical documentation. Regard runs in the background, analyzing labs, vitals, medications, and notes to flag conditions that may be present but undocumented — helping hospitalists catch diagnoses that might otherwise be missed during high-volume shifts.

**Limitations:** Focused on inpatient and hospital medicine use cases; not designed for outpatient primary care or specialty workflows. Acts as a clinical decision support tool, not a replacement for clinical judgment — all suggestions require physician evaluation, and buyers should ask the vendor directly for peer-reviewed outcome evidence and its regulatory posture rather than assuming clearance. Enterprise-only sales with no published pricing means it is not accessible to small practices or individual providers. Effectiveness depends on the quality and completeness of existing chart data. Still independent and operating as of mid-2026 following a $61M Series B.

## Phreesia

- **Function**: healthcare
- **Also useful for**: administrative, customer-service
- **Pricing**: Paid (quote-only subscription scaled to size and modules; reported from ~$300/mo per practice)
- **URL**: https://www.phreesia.com
- **Verified**: 2026-08-27
- **Confidence**: medium

> Patient intake automation platform with AI-powered scheduling, digital check-in, insurance verification, and pre-visit workflows.

**Best for:** Medical practices and health systems looking to reduce front-desk administrative burden and improve the patient arrival experience. Phreesia handles digital check-in, consent forms, copay collection, real-time insurance eligibility verification, and appointment scheduling — replacing manual intake processes that typically consume significant staff time per patient visit.

**Limitations:** Primarily addresses the administrative and operational side of healthcare, not clinical workflows. Phreesia quotes a tailored subscription rather than a published rate, and add-on modules such as texting, reminders, payments, and AI features can push a small practice from roughly $300/mo toward $800/mo or more — confirm exactly what the quote includes. ROI depends on how much manual intake work it actually displaces. Customization of intake forms and workflows requires upfront configuration effort, and patient adoption varies — some demographics still prefer paper-based check-in.
