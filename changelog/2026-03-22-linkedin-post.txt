jobsdata.ai Weekly Update -- Week of March 15-22, 2026

Yale Budget Lab's latest CPS analysis dropped this week: January/February 2026 data shows zero measurable AI labor market displacement. Occupational dissimilarity flat, exposure quintiles stable. Meanwhile, an Oxford Internet Institute study finds a 23% wage premium for AI skills across 10M+ UK job vacancies -- and a Gartner survey shows 20% of customer service leaders have already made AI-driven staffing cuts. The gap between "nothing is changing" and "everything is changing" keeps widening.

What's new this week:

Data movements

- Evidence tier corrections: Chegg and Duolingo corporate data downgraded from Tier 1 to Tier 2-3 (corporate proxies, not peer-reviewed research)

Site updates
- Task visualizer expanded from 67 to 114 occupations covering ~67% of US employment -- added fast food workers, admin assistants, police officers, carpenters, welders, and 42 more

By the numbers: 99 commits, 76 files changed, 7 new sources, 4 data points, 13 overlay signals, 47 new occupation profiles

The observed-vs-projected split is the most important visualization change since launch. When every Tier 1 source measuring actual employment finds ~0% displacement while projections cluster around 3-14%, the honest thing to do is show both clearly and let the reader sit with the tension. That's what the data demands right now: not resolution, but visible uncertainty.
