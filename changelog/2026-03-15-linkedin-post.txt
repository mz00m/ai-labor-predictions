jobsdata.ai Weekly Update -- Launch Week (March 8-15, 2026)

This week we launched jobsdata.ai with a new interactive Job Task Visualizer that breaks 34 occupations into their component tasks and models when AI becomes cheaper than human labor for each one. It uses a sigmoid crossover function against real compute costs, with BLS wage data as the baseline. You can adjust the assumptions yourself. The answer is not "soon" or "never" -- it depends entirely on the job, the task mix, and the deployment overhead. That specificity is the point.

What's new this week:

Interactive tools
-> Job Task Visualizer (jobsdata.ai/task-visualizer): Break any of 34 jobs into tasks, see AI capability scores, compute costs, and projected automation crossover dates. Adjust sliders to test your own assumptions.
-> US Economy view: Macro automation exposure across 160M US workers by income tier and gender, using BLS OEWS May 2024 data. Clickable income tier cards filter the workforce chart. 22 SOC occupation groups mapped.

Research and data
-> 407 verified sources ingested across all evidence tiers (219 peer-reviewed, 150 institutional, 28 major press, 10 commentary)
-> METR retraction: The widely-cited finding that AI slows experienced developers by 19% has been walked back by METR themselves. Their follow-up study with 57 developers found severe selection bias invalidated the original result. New data actually shows likely speedup. We have updated the site accordingly.
-> Shen and Tamkin (2026): Early-career workers using AI tools form skills more slowly, a finding with real workforce development implications
-> 39 papers ingested in a single batch covering displacement, wages, adoption, and exposure across 11 prediction graphs
-> Platform earnings data from Upwork and Fiverr FY2025 now feed the freelancer rate impact graph

-> Tech sector displacement: METR overlay flipped from bearish to bullish signal following retraction of the 19% slowdown finding
-> Healthcare admin: Trinity Health RCM layoffs and Google/BIDMC diagnostic AI study add real-world signal to what has been mostly forecast data

We built jobsdata.ai because the conversation about AI and jobs needs more signal and less noise. The site now tracks 18 prediction graphs across displacement, wages, adoption, and exposure, each backed by peer-reviewed research, government data, and institutional analysis with explicit evidence tiers. The Job Task Visualizer is our attempt to move past sector-level generalization and into the task-level specificity that actually matters for workforce planning. If you work in economic mobility, workforce development, or labor policy, we built this for you.
