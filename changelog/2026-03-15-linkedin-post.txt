jobsdata.ai Weekly Update -- Launch Week (March 8-15, 2026)

This week we shipped jobsdata.ai from scratch: 18 interactive prediction graphs tracking AI's real impact on the labor market, backed by 407 verified research sources. The weighted average across 14 displacement estimates sits at roughly 3% of US jobs affected by 2030. The measured impact so far: approximately 0%. The gap between forecast and observation is the story this dashboard exists to track.

What's new this week:

Research and data
- 407 sources ingested across all evidence tiers (219 peer-reviewed, 150 think tank/institutional, 28 major press, 10 commentary)
- Notable: Becker/METR RCT finds AI slows experienced developers by 19%, challenging the assumption that AI uniformly boosts productivity. Shen and Tamkin (2026) show early-career workers using AI tools form skills more slowly -- a finding with real workforce development implications.
- 39 papers ingested in a single batch covering displacement, wages, adoption, and exposure across 11 prediction graphs
- Data from Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank, Upwork and Fiverr FY2025 earnings, Trinity Health RCM layoffs, and Google/BIDMC diagnostic AI study

Data movements
- Overall US displacement consensus: approximately -3% by 2030, weighted across 14 Tier 1-3 estimates
- Healthcare admin displacement: Trinity Health RCM layoffs and Google/BIDMC diagnostic AI study add real-world signal to what has been mostly forecast data
- Freelancer rate impact: Upwork and Fiverr earnings data provide first platform-level revenue signals for gig economy exposure

Site updates
- Job Task Visualizer: new interactive tool at jobsdata.ai/task-visualizer that breaks 34 occupations into automatable task components, models compute costs, and projects when AI becomes cheaper than human labor using a sigmoid crossover function
- US Economy view: macro automation impact across 160 million workers by income tier, using BLS OEWS May 2024 data with gender exposure analysis
- Full-text search, reading list page, study submission form, and a scrolling source ticker now live
- Color system and chart design overhauled for accessibility and visual clarity

By the numbers: 151 commits, 2,082 files changed, 407 sources verified, 18 prediction graphs live

The site exists because the conversation about AI and jobs is dominated by two camps -- techno-optimists who see only productivity gains and doomers who see only displacement. The evidence, as usual, is more complicated. Two findings from this week capture the tension: AI makes some workers measurably more productive (the median across 18 studies is a 21% boost), but experienced developers using AI actually shipped code 19% slower in a controlled trial. Context matters. Job type matters. Implementation matters. That is what this dashboard is designed to surface.
