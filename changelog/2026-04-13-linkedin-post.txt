We built an automated fact-checker for jobsdata.ai this week. It found errors in our own data.

One example: we had 6 data points showing tech sector jobs declining — attributed to a BLS study that actually projects software developer employment to increase 17.9% through 2033. The exact opposite of what we were displaying.

Another: a key displacement estimate from Acemoglu was off by 10x. We had 5%. The paper says 0.5%. His whole thesis is that AI displacement effects are limited.

Both errors survived months of manual review. An automated agent caught them in one pass — auditing every URL, every data point, every weighted average across 513 sources.

That's the story of this week. Not new research. Integrity.

Here's what we shipped:

**Data integrity:**
- 7 critical data errors corrected
- 17 broken URLs fixed (15 from a single source that silently truncated)
- 6 stale dashboard values synced — one was off by 4.9 percentage points
- White-collar displacement chart fully restructured for clarity

**AI Action Plan improvements:**
- Users can now give feedback between assessment steps — priorities, adjustments, context
- New "Skills That Grow With AI" section in reports
- Full risk dimensions now in PDF export
- Nav renamed from "Dashboard" to "Action Plan" — because that's what it is

**New infrastructure:**
- Automated research agent scans for new sources weekly
- Automated fact-check agent audits the full dataset monthly
- Both open PRs for human review. No auto-publishing.

**New research:**
- OpenAI's industrial policy paper — notable for one line: "Workers using AI might well agree that it's increasing their productivity without believing they're seeing the benefits." We added it as a signal on the wage impact graphs.

The uncomfortable lesson: if you're building a data product and you haven't automated verification, you don't know what's wrong with your data. We didn't.
